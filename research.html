Research

My research studies economic behavior as grounded in cognitive and biological processes. It combines economic theory with experimental methods and evidence from neuroscience and genetics. Across different stages of my work, I have studied decision making, strategic interaction, cognition, and more recently the role of genetic and environmental factors in shaping economic outcomes.

Decision theory and game theory

My work in decision theory and game theory studies preferences, uncertainty, and equilibrium in dynamic and strategic settings. It includes research on unawareness, ambiguity, representation of preferences, and equilibrium behavior in large games and economies with incomplete information.

Behavioral and experimental economics

My work in behavioral and experimental economics examines how incentives, social preferences, and cognitive factors influence individual behavior. This research includes studies of motivation, competition, cooperation, intelligence, personality, and the determinants of economic choices and outcomes.

Neuroeconomics

My research in neuroeconomics combines economic theory with neuroscience to study the neural mechanisms underlying decision making. This work includes experimental and imaging studies of valuation, information processing, adaptation, and the neural representation of economic choice.

Genetics and economic outcomes

More recent work integrates genetic and environmental factors into the study of economic behavior and outcomes. This research examines educational attainment, intergenerational mobility, and the interaction between cognitive and non-cognitive traits and economic performance, often using family-based data and polygenic scores.
